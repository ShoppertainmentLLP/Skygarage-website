Exhaust work divides cleanly into two categories: cheap noise problems, and expensive emissions
problems. Telling them apart takes about ten minutes on a lift.

## The noise half

A rasp on cold start that quietens as the car warms is usually a small leak at a joint, where hot
metal expands and closes the gap. A rattle over bumps is very often a heat shield or a perished
rubber mounting. A drone at a particular speed is frequently a mounting letting the system touch
the body.

All of those are inexpensive. None of them require a new exhaust system, and being quoted one for
any of them is worth a second opinion.

## The emissions half

Catalytic converters are the expensive part, and they rarely fail on their own. They are
destroyed by what passes through them: unburnt fuel from a misfire, or oil from worn valve stem
seals or piston rings.

That matters because fitting a new converter to a car that is still misfiring is buying the same
part twice. Any quote for a converter should come with an explanation of why the first one died.

Oxygen sensors are the other common item, and they degrade rather than fail outright. A lazy
sensor still reports plausible values but responds too slowly, which costs fuel economy long
before it sets a code.

## Before the annual test

A blowing exhaust and a catalyst efficiency fault will both fail the UAE inspection, and both are
cheaper to sort out on your own timetable than in the queue on renewal day.
