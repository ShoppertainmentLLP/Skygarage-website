Window tinting in Dubai is bought for one reason and sold on another. People buy it for heat.
Most of the market sells it on shade — and shade is fixed by law at 50% visible light
transmission for a private car, so it is the one variable you cannot actually change.

What you are choosing is the film technology, and it is where the price range comes from.

## Dyed, carbon, ceramic

Dyed film is the cheap option and the reason for every purple car you have seen on Sheikh Zayed
Road. UV breaks the dye down, the colour shifts, the adhesive fails, and it bubbles. In this
climate that takes about two summers.

Carbon film does not fade and does not interfere with signals. It rejects a reasonable amount of
heat and is the sensible budget choice.

Ceramic film is the one that earns its price here. At the same legal 50% shade it blocks far more
infrared — the part of sunlight you feel as heat on your arm — so the cabin cools faster after
the car has sat in a car park and the AC stops fighting the glass. On a car you keep, the
difference is noticeable every single day from May to October.

## The windscreen matters more than people expect

The largest piece of glass on the car, angled up at the sun, is the one most people leave alone
because they think tinting it is illegal. Clear heat-rejection windscreen film at 70%+ VLT is
legal and is the single most effective piece of glass to treat. It also slows the dashboard
cracking that eventually happens to every car parked outside here.

## What separates a good job from a cheap one

The film is maybe half the cost. The rest is preparation, and it is visible for the life of the
car.

Glass is cleaned twice, because the second clean is what removes what the first one loosened.
Patterns are computer-cut to the model rather than trimmed against the rubbers with a blade. The
rear screen is heat-shrunk to a compound curve, which is the step that separates a clean rear
window from one with creases fanning out of the corners. And any old film is fully stripped,
adhesive included — leaving residue guarantees the new film fails early.

None of that is visible in a price. It is visible in the finished car, which is why the reviews
on this page are attached to the specific job.

## Getting it checked

Ask the installer to put a VLT meter on the finished glass and show you the number. It takes ten
seconds, it settles any argument at the annual inspection, and an installer who will not do it is
telling you something.
