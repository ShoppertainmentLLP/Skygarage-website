Tyres are the only part of a car touching the road, and in the UAE they are the component most
often replaced too late for a reason that has nothing to do with tread depth.

## Age, not wear

Rubber degrades. Heat accelerates it, and sustained 45°C ambient with road surfaces far hotter
than that accelerates it a great deal. A tyre with 5 mm of tread and a 2019 date code is a tyre
whose carcass has spent six summers here, and the standard local advice — replace at five years
regardless — exists because of exactly that.

This is also why the date code is worth as much attention as the price. Tyres sit in warehouses.
A heavily discounted set that was manufactured three years ago has already used more than half of
its practical life before it touches your car.

## Pressure in summer

Pressure rises as tyres heat up. That is normal, designed for, and not a reason to bleed air out
at a petrol station at 3pm.

Setting pressures cold, to the figure on the door plate, is what the car was engineered around.
Under-inflation is what causes sidewall flex, heat build-up and the summer blowouts that happen
on the Abu Dhabi road every year.

## When a puncture is repairable

In the central tread area, under roughly 6 mm across, with an internal patch-plug — yes, properly
and safely. In the shoulder or the sidewall — no, at any price, by anyone.

## The other half of the job

New tyres want balancing, and a car that chewed the last set unevenly wants aligning before the
new ones go on. Both are cheap next to a set of tyres, and skipping them is how a new set wears
out in half the distance it should.
