Paint work is judged years after it is done, in daylight, by someone deciding what your car is
worth. That is the standard worth quoting against.

## Matching a faded car

Every car in the UAE is fading. Sustained UV takes red and black furthest, but nothing escapes
it, and after four or five years the panel next to your damage is measurably not the colour it
left the factory as.

A shop that mixes strictly to the paint code produces a technically correct panel that looks
newer than the car. A shop that sprays a test card, checks it against your panel in daylight and
adjusts is doing the job people actually want.

## Blending is the whole trick

Rather than stopping the new colour at the panel edge, a good painter fades it out into the
adjacent panels so there is no line where old meets new. It costs more time and materials.

It is also the single clearest difference between a repair you can find and one you cannot, and
it is worth asking directly whether a quote includes it.

## Why the booth matters here

Dust is constant in this environment. Paint sprayed in an open yard picks up grit that ends up
suspended in the clear coat and cannot be polished out afterwards.

Booth time costs money, which is exactly why the cheapest quotes often do not include it.

## After the repair

Fresh paint keeps hardening for weeks. Wash with water only for the first fortnight, and leave
waxing, sealing or ceramic coating for at least a month — sealing it early traps solvents and
dulls the finish you paid for.
