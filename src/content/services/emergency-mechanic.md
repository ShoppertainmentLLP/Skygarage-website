An emergency mechanic exists to answer one question quickly: is this a twenty-minute repair or a
recovery?

Getting that answer at the roadside rather than after a tow changes both the cost and your
position in whatever comes next.

## Most breakdowns are small

The cars that stop on Dubai roads mostly stop for mundane reasons. A battery at the end of its
life in its third summer. An alternator that stopped charging. A snapped auxiliary belt. A split
coolant hose. A failed coil putting the engine into limp mode. A relay.

Every one of those is fixable where the car is, with the right van and the right parts on board.
Paying for a recovery first means paying for a tow you did not need and then paying a workshop
for a job that took twenty minutes.

## When it is not small

Sometimes it is a gearbox, a timing chain, or an engine that has been driven while overheating.
A good roadside mechanic tells you that within the first twenty minutes and stops.

What you should still get is a written diagnosis, because that is what lets you choose a garage
and compare quotes rather than being delivered to whichever workshop the tow truck prefers.

## The overheating rule

Worth repeating on its own. If the temperature gauge climbs or a coolant warning appears, pull
over and stop. Do not try to make it to the next exit, and do not open the cap while it is hot.

The difference between stopping immediately and driving another ten minutes is very often the
difference between a AED 400 hose and a AED 9,000 engine repair.

## While you wait

Hazards on, car as far off the carriageway as you can get it, and if you are on a motorway,
everyone out and behind the barrier. Nothing about the car is worth standing next to it on a hard
shoulder here.
