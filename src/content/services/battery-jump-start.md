A jump start is a small job with one big question attached: will the car start again tomorrow?

## Why it went flat matters more than the start

There are only a few realistic causes.

The battery is at the end of its life, which in the UAE arrives at two to three years rather than
the four or five people expect. Sustained heat degrades a lead-acid battery far faster than cold
does.

The alternator is not charging it, in which case the car is running off the battery until it
stops — often somewhere considerably less convenient than where it started.

Something is draining it overnight. Aftermarket alarms, trackers and dash cameras installed into
permanently live circuits are the usual suspects here.

Or the driving pattern never replaces what starting uses: short trips in traffic, day after day,
which is a very common Dubai profile.

A load test and a charging check take five minutes and tell you which of those you have. That is
why they are part of the call-out rather than an extra.

## Basements

Worth mentioning when you call. A lot of tower car parks have height restrictions that a standard
recovery vehicle cannot clear, and the unit that can get to you is not always the first one
available. Saying "level B3, Marina" at the start saves a wasted trip.

## Doing it yourself

If you have leads and a willing neighbour, connect positive to positive, then negative on the
donor car to an unpainted engine earth point on yours — not to the flat battery's negative
terminal. Modern cars dislike voltage spikes, and a reversed connection can take out modules that
cost far more than a call-out.
