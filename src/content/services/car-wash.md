Washing a car in Dubai is not the same maintenance job it is elsewhere, and the price you pay
has almost nothing to do with how dirty the car looks.

Three things work against paint here. Fine desert dust settles on every surface within a day of
a wash and holds overnight humidity against the clear coat. Summer sun bakes whatever is sitting
on the paint — bird lime, tree sap, sprinkler water — hard enough that it etches. And the water
itself is hard, so a car left to air-dry in the sun ends up with mineral spots that a later wash
will not lift.

None of that makes washing complicated. It does mean **how** the car is washed matters more here
than the frequency does. A brush tunnel at AED 30 will clean the car and put a fresh set of fine
swirl marks into the paint every time; on black or dark grey they show up as a haze under direct
sun within a few months. A two-bucket hand wash costs a little more, takes fifteen minutes
longer, and does not.

## What you are actually paying for

The gap between a AED 25 wash and a AED 150 one is time and decontamination, not soap.

A maintenance wash removes what is loosely sitting on the car. It is the right job most weeks,
and paying more for it is money wasted. An inside-and-out wash adds the interior properly —
seat rails, boot lip, door shuts, the console — which is where sand collects and where a quick
wash stops. A full clean adds a clay bar to pull out the bonded contamination a wash cannot
touch, then a sealant that makes the next few washes easier.

Most people over-buy the middle option and under-buy the top one. If the car has not been
decontaminated in a year, one clay-and-seal visit does more for how it looks than six premium
washes.

## Size and colour change the price

Every bay in Dubai charges more for a seven-seat SUV than a saloon, usually 30–50% more, and
that is fair — it is more surface, more glass and more interior. What is not always fair is
finding out at the counter. The quotes you get through Cars911 are for your car, because the
request includes the make and model.

Dark colours are also more expensive to keep looking right, not to wash. Black shows every swirl
and every water spot, which is why black cars in this city tend to end up on a hand-wash-only
rule and, eventually, on ceramic coating.

## When a wash is not the answer

If the paint feels rough to a fingertip through a plastic bag, it needs decontamination. If it
looks hazy under direct sun, it needs a machine polish. If you are washing weekly and still
fighting water spots and dust, a ceramic coating changes the economics — the car stays cleaner
between washes and the washes get quicker.

A wash bay will tell you that. The good ones tell you before you have paid for four washes that
were never going to fix it.
