Brakes are the easiest job on a car to over-sell, because the customer never sees the numbers
that justify the work.

Every brake pad has a friction-material thickness, measured in millimetres. Every disc has a
minimum thickness stamped into its hub face. Those two figures decide the entire job. A workshop
that measures them and shows you is quoting; one that says "your brakes are finished" is
selling.

## What Dubai does to brakes

Heat, mostly. Braking converts motion into heat, and a system that starts at 45°C ambient has
far less capacity to shed it than one starting at 15°C. That shows up as faster pad wear, more
disc distortion, and brake fluid that degrades sooner.

Fluid is the one people forget. It is hygroscopic — it pulls moisture out of the air through the
hoses and reservoir over time — and moisture lowers its boiling point. Fluid that boils gives you
a pedal that goes to the floor at exactly the moment you need it not to. It is a AED 199 job on
a two-year cycle and it is not optional here.

The other local factor is corrosion on cars near the coast. Calliper slider pins seize, one pad
does all the work, and the disc overheats on one side. Cleaning and greasing the hardware at
every brake service prevents it, and plenty of quick-fit places skip it.

## Reading the symptom

A squeal under light braking is usually the wear indicator — a small metal tab designed to make
that noise. Book it in, no panic.

Grinding is metal on metal. The pad is gone and every stop is cutting into the disc, turning a
pads job into pads and discs. Do not drive it further than the garage.

A pulsing pedal is disc runout. A soft or slowly sinking pedal is hydraulic — a leak, a failing
master cylinder or boiled fluid — and it is the one that deserves genuine urgency.

## Where the quotes differ

Parts, mostly. Genuine pads, OEM pads from the company that made the originals, and quality
aftermarket are three real tiers with real price differences, and for most cars the middle one
is the right answer. What should never differ is whether the hardware was serviced and whether
the car was road tested afterwards.
