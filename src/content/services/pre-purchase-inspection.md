A pre-purchase inspection is the highest-return spend on this site. A few hundred dirhams
regularly moves a five-figure decision.

## What sellers do not mention

Repaired accident damage, mostly.

Cars change hands quickly in this market and body repair is good and cheap here, which is an
excellent combination for sellers and a poor one for buyers. A well-repaired panel looks
identical to an original one under a showroom light.

It does not measure the same. A paint depth gauge across every panel produces a set of readings
in seconds, and a panel at three times factory thickness has been resprayed, whatever the advert
says.

## The scan is not optional

Sellers clear fault codes before a viewing. Everybody knows this and the codes come back after a
drive cycle, which is after you have paid.

What clearing does not erase is readiness status — a car with every monitor showing "not ready"
had its codes cleared very recently, which is a fact worth knowing. Nor does it erase mileage
records stored in other modules, which frequently disagree with a wound-back cluster.

## The road test

Cold start matters most and is the part most often skipped, because sellers warm the car up
before you arrive. If you can, arrange to see it cold.

Then: motorway speed for vibration, hard braking for judder, full lock for CV and steering
noises, and the AC on maximum in the heat, which is where a weak system reveals itself.

## Using the report

The findings come with repair costs, and that column is what makes them useful. A car needing
AED 6,000 of work is not necessarily a car to avoid — it is a car worth AED 6,000 less, and now
you can say so with evidence.
