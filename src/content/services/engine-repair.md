Engine work is the highest-stakes category on this site. It has the widest price range, the most
uncertainty, and by some distance the most scope for a wrong diagnosis to cost thousands.

## Testing is not optional

Before anyone has a defensible opinion about an engine, four things should have happened: a
compression test, a leak-down test, a cooling system pressure test, and a full scan with live
data.

Those tests distinguish worn rings from a leaking valve, a head gasket from an external coolant
leak, and a genuine mechanical fault from a sensor telling lies. They take an hour or two. They
routinely change a quote by an order of magnitude.

Any workshop willing to price a rebuild without doing them is pricing its own uncertainty, and
you are paying for it.

## Heat is usually in the story

Almost every catastrophic engine failure seen in this country traces back to cooling. A hose
weeps, the level drops, the car runs slightly hot for weeks, the head warps, and eventually the
gasket goes.

The practical lesson is unglamorous: take temperature warnings seriously and stop the car. The
difference between pulling over immediately and driving another twenty minutes is frequently the
difference between a AED 900 repair and a AED 9,000 one.

## Getting a second price

On a five-figure quote, comparing is simply sensible. Ask for the diagnostic findings in writing
— the compression figures, the leak-down percentages, the scan report — and take them to another
workshop for a price on the same defined work.

Two quotes for "engine rebuild" are not comparable. Two quotes against the same test results are.
