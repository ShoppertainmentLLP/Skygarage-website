A wrap is a full-body vinyl covering, and in Dubai it is bought for two quite different reasons:
to change the colour of a car without painting it, and to protect the paint of a car that will be
sold later.

## Cast film, not calendered

This is the single decision that determines whether a wrap looks good in three years.

Cast vinyl is manufactured in a way that leaves it dimensionally stable, so it conforms to curves
and stays where it is put. Calendered film is stretched during manufacture and wants to return to
that shape, which in UAE heat means shrinking back from edges and recesses.

That difference is invisible on the day and unmistakable a year later, when a cheap wrap starts
showing lines of original paint at every panel edge.

## The preparation is the same as paint

Vinyl does not hide anything. It follows the surface underneath exactly, so a dent or a rough
repair shows through, and contamination left on the paint stops the adhesive bonding.

Handles, badges and some trim should come off rather than be trimmed around. It costs more hours
and it is the difference between a car that looks like it was made that colour and one that looks
covered.

## Living with matte and satin

Worth thinking about before you choose. Matte and satin finishes look superb and are noticeably
harder to keep clean — they show fingerprints around the handles, they cannot be polished if
something marks them, and they need dedicated cleaning products because normal wax leaves shiny
patches.

## The paperwork

A colour change must be declared and the vehicle registration updated. It is routine, installers
here handle it constantly, and skipping it causes problems at inspection and with insurance
claims.
