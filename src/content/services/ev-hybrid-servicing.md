Electric cars need less servicing than combustion cars and a completely different kind of
attention. Both halves of that sentence get lost in practice.

## The battery is the car

Its state of health is the number that determines what the car is worth, how far it goes, and
whether it is a good used buy. Reading it properly requires manufacturer-level diagnostic access
rather than a generic tool.

Having it recorded annually creates a trend line, and a documented trend is worth real money at
resale in a market where buyers are still nervous about used EVs.

## Heat, and what to do about it

Battery degradation accelerates with temperature, and this is one of the hottest markets in the
world for electric cars.

The practical mitigations are unglamorous. Park in shade or a basement where you can. Avoid
leaving the car sitting at 100% state of charge in the heat, since a high state of charge at high
temperature is the worst combination for cell ageing. Use rapid charging when you need it rather
than as a routine. And keep the thermal management system healthy — its coolant, its pump, and a
radiator that is not packed with sand — because it is the only thing actively protecting the pack.

## Brakes: the counterintuitive one

Regenerative braking means the friction brakes do very little work. Pads on a three-year-old EV
often look new.

That is exactly the problem. Callipers that rarely move seize, slider pins corrode, and discs
develop surface rust that then wears unevenly. On an electric car, brake service is about
cleaning and greasing hardware rather than replacing pads, and skipping it because "the pads are
fine" is how EVs end up with expensive calliper work.

## What it should cost

Less than a combustion service. No oil, no plugs, no fuel filters, no exhaust. A quote at
petrol-car prices is a quote for work that does not exist on your car.
