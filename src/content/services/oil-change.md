An oil change is the simplest job on this site and the one most often done to the wrong
specification.

The reason is that people ask for "5W-30" when the manufacturer asked for something narrower.
Modern engines carry approvals — BMW Longlife-01, VW 504.00, Mercedes 229.5, Porsche C30 — and
those codes describe additive chemistry and shear stability, not just how thick the oil is when
cold. Two bottles both labelled 5W-30 can be entirely different products, and only one of them
is the one your car's timing system was designed around.

## Why the interval is shorter here

Manufacturer intervals are written for average conditions. Dubai is not average.

Ambient heat above 40°C for months oxidises oil faster. Short journeys never bring it fully up to
temperature, so moisture and fuel dilution stay in the sump. And sitting in traffic runs the
engine at length while the odometer does nothing, so kilometres understate the hours the oil has
worked.

The practical answer most independent workshops here give is 10,000 km or twelve months, and
sooner for a car doing school runs and mall trips. Nobody has ever damaged an engine by changing
oil too often.

## Where the price difference is

Almost entirely in the oil itself, and how much of it the engine takes.

A four-cylinder taking four litres of a common full synthetic is a different bill from a V8
taking eight litres of an approved low-ash oil. The labour is close to identical. So when quotes
come back at AED 149 and AED 420, the useful question is which oil and how many litres — which
is why both are on the quotes here.

## The things that get skipped

The sump washer, the service indicator reset, and the filter itself. All three are small and all
three are visible later: a weeping sump plug, a car that keeps asking for a service it has had,
and old filter media in new oil.

Ask for the old filter to be left in the box on the passenger seat. Good workshops do it without
being asked.
