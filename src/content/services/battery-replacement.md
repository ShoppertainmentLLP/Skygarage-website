Car batteries in the UAE fail early, and they fail without much warning.

The reason is heat. A lead-acid battery degrades through internal corrosion and electrolyte
loss, and both accelerate sharply with temperature. A battery that would give five years in a
European climate gives two to three here, and the failure is usually abrupt: the car starts
normally on Tuesday and does not on Wednesday.

## Test first, always

The most common unnecessary purchase in this category is a battery that was fine.

A car that will not start has several possible causes: the battery, the alternator not charging
it, a parasitic drain flattening it overnight, corroded terminals, or a failing starter. A load
test and a charging-system check separate them in about fifteen minutes.

Fitting a battery to a car with a bad alternator means being stranded again in a week, having
paid for a battery you did not need and still needing the alternator.

## The registration step

This is the part that is quietly skipped most often.

Nearly every car built in the last ten years tracks the age and condition of its battery and
adjusts charging voltage to suit. When a new battery goes in, that profile has to be reset with a
diagnostic tool. If it is not, the car keeps charging as though the battery were four years old
and tired, which cooks the new one.

It takes two minutes. Ask whether it is included — on a modern European car especially, it is
not optional.

## Getting the right one

Capacity, cold-cranking amps, terminal position and type. Cars specified for AGM — most
stop-start systems and most recent European models — must have AGM, because the charging regime
and the deep-cycling would destroy a conventional battery quickly.

A quality battery costs more and lasts proportionally longer, which in a climate that shortens
every battery's life is a reasonable place to spend the money.
