Car AC is the one system in this country where a bad repair is immediately obvious and an
unnecessary one is almost impossible to spot.

The unnecessary one is the regas. Refrigerant is sealed in a closed loop and is not consumed by
use, so a system that is low on gas has a leak somewhere. Refilling it without finding the leak
produces a car that is beautifully cold for six weeks and then is not, at which point the same
garage will happily regas it again.

## What actually goes wrong here

Dubai breaks AC systems in ways temperate climates do not.

The condenser sits at the very front of the car, behind the grille, doing the job of a radiator
for the AC. Sand and dust pack into its fins over a few years and it stops shedding heat. The
system is perfect, the gas is correct, and the car still blows warm in traffic and cools on the
move. That is a cleaning job, not a parts job, and a garage that jumps to a compressor is not
looking.

Cooling fans are the second. They run constantly here and they fail, with exactly the same
symptom.

Compressors do fail, and when they do it is the expensive one. What matters then is whether the
system is flushed properly — a compressor that has broken up internally sends metal through the
loop, and a new one fitted without a flush and a new drier will fail again.

## Reading your own symptom

Cold at speed but not in traffic points at airflow across the condenser: fan or blockage.

Cold then gradually warm points at a leak, or at icing from an over-full or moisture-contaminated
system.

Cold on one side only, or the temperature not responding to the dial, is usually a blend door
actuator — a mechanical fault inside the dash and nothing to do with refrigerant at all.

Noisy when the AC clicks on is the compressor clutch or the compressor itself.

## Getting an honest quote

Ask what was measured. Any garage that gives you a price without pressures, a vent temperature
and a leak test has guessed, and the guess is usually the most expensive item on the list.

The quotes on this page come from workshops that diagnose first, and the diagnostic fee is
normally credited against the repair if you go ahead.
