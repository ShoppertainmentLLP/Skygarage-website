Transmissions produce the widest gap on this site between what a fault feels like and what it
actually is. That gap is where the money goes.

## Cheapest causes first

A gearbox that jolts, slips or hesitates has a list of possible causes, and the list is ordered
by price for a reason. Degraded fluid. A failing speed or range sensor. Worn engine and gearbox
mounts, which make normal shifts feel violent. A mechatronic or solenoid fault. An engine misfire
that the transmission is reacting to. And only then, internal wear.

The first five are all a fraction of a rebuild. A workshop that reads adaptation values, checks
fluid condition and road tests with logging rules them in or out in about an hour.

## The fluid argument

Many manufacturers describe their automatics as sealed for life. That claim was written for
markets where a gearbox does not sit at 50°C ambient in stop-start traffic for months.

The consensus among independent specialists here is a fluid and filter service every 60,000 to
80,000 km, using the specified fluid and filling to temperature rather than to a line. Done on
schedule it is a few hundred dirhams. Left until the fluid is dark and the shifts are harsh, it
is sometimes too late to help.

One caveat worth knowing: on a very high-mileage gearbox that has never been serviced, a full
flush can occasionally dislodge deposits that were holding things together. A good specialist
will suggest a partial service and set your expectations rather than pretending the risk is zero.

## Judging a rebuild quote

Ask what is replaced as standard — the torque converter and the cooler flush are the two items
that separate a lasting rebuild from a repeat visit — and read the warranty. Twelve months is
normal from a workshop confident in its work.
