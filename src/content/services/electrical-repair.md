Electrical faults are the ones where the difference between a good workshop and a bad one is most
expensive, because a bad one fixes them by buying parts.

## Why so many faults here are connections

Heat cycles metal. A car parked outside goes from 30°C at night to well over 70°C inside the
engine bay during the day, every day, and every connector expands and contracts with it. Near the
coast, humidity adds corrosion to the same joints.

The result is that a very large share of UAE electrical faults are not failed components at all.
They are a corroded earth strap, a green pin in a connector, a chafed section of loom rubbing on
a bracket. None of those set a helpful fault code, and none are found by a scan tool alone. They
are found with a meter, testing voltage drop across the circuit under load.

## The overnight flat battery

The most common single complaint in this category, and almost always a parasitic drain.

Something is staying awake. In this market it is very often an aftermarket alarm, tracker or dash
camera spliced into a permanently live circuit by whoever installed it. Occasionally it is a
module that will not sleep, or a light in the boot that never goes out.

Finding it means measuring the draw with the car locked and the modules asleep, then pulling
fuses one at a time. It takes an hour or two and it is worth every dirham, because the
alternative is a new battery every few months.

## What a lasting repair looks like

Soldered and heat-shrunk, not twisted and taped. Proper connectors. The loom secured away from
anything that moves or gets hot.

In this climate the shortcut version fails within a year, usually somewhere less convenient.
