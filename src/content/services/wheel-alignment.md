Alignment is the cheapest job on this site that saves the most money, and the one most often sold
without being done properly.

The geometry of a car — how each wheel points relative to the road and to the others — is set at
the factory within tight tolerances. Kerb a wheel coming out of a basement ramp, drop into one of
the potholes that open up on Al Khail after a rain, or take a speed bump at Dubai speed, and toe
moves. It does not need a crash. Half a degree is enough to change how the car tracks and how
fast the tyres wear.

## What you notice first

Usually not the steering. Usually the tyres.

A car out of toe scrubs rubber sideways on every rotation. The inner or outer shoulder wears
down while the rest of the tread looks fine, and a set of tyres that should last 40,000 km is
finished at 15,000. At Dubai tyre prices, that is a set of tyres to save AED 150 of alignment.

The other signs are a wheel that sits off centre when the car is going straight, and a drift when
you loosen your grip on a flat road. Both are worth checking, though a drift can also be a tyre
fault or a sticking caliper, which is why a shop that measures before adjusting is worth more
than one that does not.

## Four wheels, not two

A large part of the market sells a front-toe adjustment and calls it alignment. On a modern car
with independent rear suspension, that is half the job. If the rear axle has a thrust angle, the
car drives slightly crabbed and the front has to be set crooked to compensate — which is why some
cars come back from an alignment with a straight-driving car and a steering wheel that is still
off centre.

Ask what is measured. Ask for the printout.

## When alignment is not the answer

If a bush, ball joint or track rod end is worn, geometry will not hold — the readings will be
correct on the rig and wrong again within a week. A garage that checks the suspension first and
tells you the real answer is a repair costs you a diagnosis and saves you an alignment you would
have paid for twice.

That is the check the workshops in this network do as standard, and it is why the quotes you get
back sometimes say the alignment is not what your car needs.
