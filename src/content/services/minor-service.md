A minor service is two jobs sold as one: an oil change, and an inspection. The oil change is
routine and almost everyone does it competently. The inspection is where the money either gets
saved or quietly wasted.

## What the inspection should find

A car in Dubai deteriorates in a pattern. Coolant hoses harden and weep at the clips. Batteries
die at two to three years rather than four to five. Wiper blades perish in months. Tyres age out
before they wear out, cracking in the sidewall while the tread still looks fine. Cabin filters
clog with fine dust and choke the AC that you depend on.

None of these are dramatic and all of them are visible to someone who looks. That is the whole
argument for a service being more than an oil change.

## What it should not do

Turn into a bigger bill without a phone call.

The most common complaint about servicing anywhere is the invoice that arrives with items nobody
discussed. The workshops in this network quote findings separately and wait for approval, and
most send photographs with the report. If something genuinely needs doing while the car is
already on the ramp, you should hear the reason and the price before it happens, not after.

## Intervals that suit this climate

The book figure is a starting point. Sustained heat, short trips and long idles in traffic all
age oil and filters faster than the schedule assumes, so the practical advice here is a minor
service every 10,000 km or twelve months, whichever arrives first.

If the car does very few kilometres, the twelve months is the one that matters. Oil degrades with
time and moisture as well as with distance, and a car doing 4,000 km a year still needs an
annual change.

## Comparing quotes properly

Because the scope is defined, the numbers mean something. What still varies is the oil
specification, the number of litres and whether the cabin filter is included. Those are the three
questions worth asking when two quotes are AED 200 apart.
