A major service is the once-every-few-years appointment where the whole car is examined rather
than topped up. It is also where the price gap between a main dealer and a good independent
specialist is at its widest, for work that is genuinely the same.

## What "due" means

The right major service is not a package. It is the intersection of the manufacturer's schedule
and what your particular car has actually had done.

Plugs at the correct interval. Coolant when it has lost its inhibitor package, which in this
heat is sooner than the book often says. Transmission fluid where the manufacturer specifies it —
and note that "sealed for life" gearboxes still benefit from a fluid change in a climate where
the transmission runs hot and stop-start traffic works the torque converter constantly.

A workshop that asks what was done last time is doing the job properly. One that quotes before
asking is selling a package.

## The scan matters more than people think

Every module in a modern car stores faults, including ones that have not yet triggered a warning
light. Reading them during a major service catches the misfire that is building, the sensor that
is drifting, the module reporting low voltage overnight.

Those are cheap fixes discovered early and expensive ones discovered late.

## Where the money actually goes

Labour and parts, in that order, and the split explains most quote differences. A V8 takes eight
plugs, more oil and more time than a four-cylinder taking four. A car with the oil filter buried
under the intake takes an hour longer than one with it on top.

That is why quotes on this page are for your car and engine rather than a generic figure, and why
the ones that come back differ.

## Keeping the record

Whatever you do, keep the invoice. A documented service history — dates, mileages, specifications
— is worth real money when you sell in this market, and reconstructing it afterwards is
impossible.
