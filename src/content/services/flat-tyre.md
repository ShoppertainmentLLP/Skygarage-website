A flat tyre in this climate is more urgent than in most places, because heat turns a slow
deflation into a failure much faster.

## Safety before anything else

If you are on a motorway, get the car as far off the carriageway as you can, put the hazards on,
and get everyone out and behind the barrier. Changing a wheel on the hard shoulder of Sheikh
Zayed Road is not worth the risk of doing yourself, and the wait for a unit is short.

## What can and cannot be repaired

A puncture in the central tread area, under about 6 mm, can be repaired properly — from the
inside, with a combined patch and plug, after the tyre has been taken off the rim and inspected.

A puncture in the shoulder or sidewall cannot be repaired safely. Neither can a tyre that has
been driven on while flat, because the internal structure is damaged even when the outside looks
fine.

An external plug pushed in without removing the tyre is a temporary get-you-home measure, not a
repair, whatever it costs.

## The no-spare problem

A lot of cars sold here now carry a sealant kit rather than a spare, or run-flats and no spare at
all. Both have real limits: sealant will not fix anything large, and a run-flat that has been run
flat needs replacing rather than repairing.

Worth knowing which you have before the day you need it.

## Pressures, monthly

Almost every roadside tyre failure in this country starts as a slow leak or an old tyre rather
than a sudden event. Checking pressures cold once a month, and glancing at the sidewalls for fine
cracking while you do, prevents most of them.
