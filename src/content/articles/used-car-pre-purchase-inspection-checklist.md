A used car may appear attractive but can conceal latent mechanical, electronic or documentation issues that only materialise after the title has transferred. In Dubai's fast-paced vehicle market, a pre-purchase inspection is essential due diligence for an asset whose condition cannot be reliably judged by appearance, mileage or a cursory test drive.

The objective is not to warrant perfection. It is to clarify what is being bought, anticipate potential liabilities, and substantiate the seller's representations through technical assessment.

## Identity, history and paperwork

The chassis number must correspond with the vehicle, the registration card and all supporting documentation. Review the service history for continuity, authorised service providers and consistency with odometer readings. An absence of invoices is not evidence of impropriety, but it does restrict your ability to verify maintenance.

Verify accident and insurance history, encumbrances, recall status, title and ownership records, and inspection data through official or duly authorised sources. Distinguish between a seller's representations and independently verifiable facts — they do not carry equal weight.

## Evidence of previous repair

Panel gaps, paint-depth discrepancies, overspray, welding marks, disturbed fasteners and inconsistent sealant may indicate prior repairs. The pertinent question is not merely whether the vehicle was repainted, but the rationale, the location and the standard of the work performed. Cosmetic refinishing may be benign, whereas structural repairs affecting pillars, chassis rails, crumple zones or mounting points warrant heightened scrutiny.

Flood exposure should also be considered. Indicators include corrosion in atypical locations, persistent damp odours, residue beneath carpets, discoloured electronic connectors and unexplained electrical faults — all of which may be concealed by cosmetic presentation.

## Start it cold

Wherever practicable, start the engine from cold. Difficult ignition, smoke, abnormal noises, unstable idling, overheating, warning indicators and fluid contamination may manifest exclusively before the vehicle reaches operating temperature.

Inspect for leaks, cooling system integrity, belts, hoses, mounts and other components subject to regular maintenance. Transmission engagement should be smooth and consistent under load; hesitation, shuddering, slippage or abrupt gear transitions require professional diagnosis and should not be accepted as characteristic of the model.

## Scan every module, not just the engine

Modern vehicles may retain diagnostic fault codes even with no warning light illuminated. A comprehensive scan should encompass all relevant control modules, not solely engine management. Test each system individually: airbags, ABS, driver-assistance features, parking sensors, cameras, climate control, infotainment, powered seats, charging functions and keyless entry.

Recently cleared codes or incomplete readiness monitors are grounds for further inquiry, particularly where the seller cannot account for a system reset.

Air-conditioning performance is essential in Dubai, but vent temperature alone is not a sufficient assessment. Evaluate compressor operation, fan functionality, refrigerant integrity and cooling stability under continuous thermal load.

Inspect tyres for manufacturing date, cracking, irregular wear and compliance with manufacturer specifications. Test the battery's health independently rather than relying on a single successful start. Sand, ambient heat and frequent stop-start operation accelerate wear on filters, rubber components, cooling systems, suspension and braking assemblies.

For imported vehicles, establish parts availability, regional specification compliance, warranty applicability and software support before signing.

## The road test

A comprehensive road test should evaluate acceleration, braking, steering at multiple speeds, gearbox function, vibration, noise, alignment and thermal behaviour. The vehicle must track in a straight line and brake without pulling, pulsation or instability.

Hybrid and electric vehicles require additional diligence on battery health, charging protocols, thermal management, high-voltage alerts and manufacturer-specific diagnostics.

The final inspection report should categorise defects by urgency and set out realistic repair implications, so you can negotiate, proceed subject to conditions, or walk away.

## What an inspection is not

A pre-purchase inspection is not a warranty against future defects, and an RTA roadworthiness certificate is not a substitute for an inspection you commission yourself. Roadworthiness at a single point in time is a different question from component lifespan, repair quality or projected ownership costs.

The most secure transaction documents the vehicle's identity, provenance, structural integrity, system functionality and anticipated expenses before payment and transfer of title. Once ownership changes, your negotiating leverage is materially diminished.
