Range Rovers are the most common luxury SUV on Dubai roads and, by some distance, the most
commonly misdiagnosed.

The reason is that the expensive systems on these cars — air suspension, cooling, electronics —
all produce vague symptoms that overlap. A car sitting low, a warning message, an intermittent
fault: each of those has four or five plausible causes at wildly different prices. A workshop
without the right diagnostic access has no way to tell them apart, so it quotes the biggest one.

## Air suspension is the headline

It is also where most owners lose money unnecessarily.

The system has a compressor, a dryer, a valve block, four air springs and four height sensors.
Any one of them will drop a corner overnight. Replacing the whole system when a AED 300 sensor
had failed is a bad afternoon, and it happens often enough that it is worth asking any quoting
garage a direct question: what did you measure, and what does the data say failed?

## Cooling, again

Like most performance-derived engines in this heat, the supercharged V6 and V8 push their cooling
systems hard. Thermostat housings weep, water pumps fail, and the first real symptom is often a
smell rather than a gauge reading. Catching it costs a fraction of what a head gasket does.

## The honest version of running one

These are not cheap cars to maintain and no independent workshop will make them so. What a good
specialist does is stop you paying for the wrong repair, offer OEM alternatives where the genuine
part is a badge tax, and tell you which items can wait a service and which cannot.

That is the difference the quotes on this page are meant to expose.
