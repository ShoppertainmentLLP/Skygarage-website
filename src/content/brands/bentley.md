Bentley servicing is mostly patience and parts pricing. The mechanical work is well within the
reach of a good specialist; the difference between workshops is what they charge for the
components and whether they tell you where alternatives exist.

## Line by line

On a car where a service can run five figures, an itemised quote is not a formality. Twelve coil
packs and twelve plugs on a W12 is a large parts bill and a long labour line, and both should be
visible.

Ask for genuine and OEM options where they exist. Modern Bentleys share a meaningful amount of
their engineering with other Volkswagen Group vehicles, and a workshop that knows which parts
those are can produce a quote that is dramatically lower without compromising anything.

Anything genuinely specific to the marque will be expensive, and honest quoting makes that
distinction rather than applying a badge multiplier to everything.

## Air suspension

The same story as every large luxury car here. Struts, compressor, valve block and sensors fail
individually and cost very different amounts. Diagnosis before replacement is where the money is
saved.

## Cars that sit

Bentleys in this market often cover very few kilometres, and low use has its own costs. Brake
discs corrode, tyres age out, batteries fail, and fluids degrade with time regardless of
distance.

An annual inspection is more useful than a mileage-based schedule for a car like this.
