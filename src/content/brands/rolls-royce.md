Rolls-Royce work in Dubai is done by a very short list of workshops, and the list is short for
practical reasons rather than prestige ones.

## The four requirements

Manufacturer-level diagnostic access, because these cars are as software-defined as any modern
luxury vehicle. Lifting and handling equipment rated for the weight. Insurance cover appropriate
to the value of a car sitting in the workshop. And secure, discreet storage.

A general workshop can be excellent and still fail on all four. The audit for this make checks
each one.

## What is underneath

Modern Rolls-Royce shares a great deal of engineering with BMW's largest platforms. That is not a
criticism — it is useful information, because it means a workshop that understands the
relationship can source a meaningful share of components sensibly rather than treating every part
as bespoke.

Anything genuinely marque-specific is expensive and slow, and should be identified as such when
the quote is written.

## Cars that barely move

Many of these cars here cover a few thousand kilometres a year, and standing still causes its own
list of problems: brake discs corroding, tyres ageing out with full tread, batteries failing,
fluids degrading on time rather than distance.

For a low-use car, an annual inspection is worth more than a mileage-triggered service, and any
specialist worth using will say so rather than waiting for the odometer.

## Planning around parts

Confirm availability before the car is booked in. Common service items are stocked; model-specific
components can take weeks, and discovering that after a car is dismantled is avoidable.
