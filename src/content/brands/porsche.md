Porsches are precise cars with a small number of well-documented weak points, which makes
specialist servicing unusually good value: the specialist knows what to look for, and looking is
cheap.

## Diagnostic access first

Almost everything beyond an oil change on a modern Porsche requires manufacturer-level software.
Adaptations, calibrations after suspension work, coding replacement modules, reading the systems
that a generic scanner cannot see.

That is the qualifying question for any workshop quoting on one of these, and it is the reason
requests for this make in the network go only to garages that hold it.

## Cooling, again

The pattern that repeats across performance cars in this climate. Coolant pipes on certain V8
models and water pumps across the range are known items, and the cost difference between planned
replacement and roadside failure is substantial.

A specialist checks for the ones that apply to your engine and VIN rather than waiting for a
symptom.

## Track use is a different car

If the car goes to Dubai Autodrome, brake fluid, engine oil and brake pads become consumables
measured in sessions rather than kilometres. Tyres too.

The workshops here will set a schedule around how you actually use the car rather than the
generic one, which for a track-used Porsche is the difference between reliability and an
expensive weekend.

## Buying used

A pre-purchase inspection on a Cayenne or Panamera is among the highest-return inspections
available — air suspension, cooling and transmission history are all things that determine
whether a car is a bargain or a project.
