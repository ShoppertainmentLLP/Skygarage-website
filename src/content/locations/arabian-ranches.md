Arabian Ranches runs on large family vehicles that live outside, do short trips during the week,
and occasionally get taken somewhere far more demanding at the weekend.

## Two driving patterns in one car

Monday to Friday is school runs and the supermarket: short journeys where the engine never fully
warms, the battery never fully recharges and the brakes never get hot enough to clear themselves.

Then a weekend run to Abu Dhabi, or out into the sand.

Neither pattern matches the schedule in the handbook, and together they mean the calendar is a
better guide than the odometer. Annual servicing regardless of distance is the sensible default
for a Ranches car.

## Parked in the sun

The villa driveway is the biggest single difference between a car here and the same car in a
Downtown basement. UV works on the paint every day, the cabin reaches temperatures that harden
plastics and crack dashboards, and the battery never gets a cool night.

The mitigations are cheap and effective: wash regularly rather than occasionally, use a
windscreen shade, and have the battery load-tested each year once it is past two.

## Sand

Enough cars here leave tarmac that it is worth naming properly. Air filters choke quickly. The
underbody collects sand in places that hold moisture. Cooling systems work hard at low speed
where there is little airflow.

After any real desert trip, an underbody look and an air filter check are worth more than
anything else you could spend the same money on.

## How the work happens

Nothing automotive operates inside the community, so every job is either collected or mobile.
Both are routine here, and for most residents the car simply disappears from the driveway in the
morning and comes back clean in the afternoon.
