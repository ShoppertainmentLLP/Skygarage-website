Dubai is an unusual car market: the vehicles are concentrated in residential towers and villa
communities, and the workshops are concentrated in four industrial areas nowhere near them.

That single geographic fact explains most of how servicing works here. Pick and drop is normal
rather than premium. Mobile mechanics are a mainstream option rather than an emergency one. And
the garage nearest your home is very rarely the right one to use.

## What the climate does

The maintenance schedule that came with your car was written for somewhere else.

Batteries fail at two to three years rather than four or five, because heat degrades them far
faster than cold does. Air conditioning is a maintenance system here rather than a comfort one.
Brake fluid absorbs moisture and wants replacing every two years regardless of mileage. Tyres age
out with legal tread still on them, cracking in the sidewall after about five summers.

Meanwhile short trips and long idles in traffic age engine oil faster than the odometer suggests,
which is why most independents here recommend 10,000 km rather than the 15,000 or 20,000 in the
book.

## The price spread

Nowhere in the country rewards comparison as much as Dubai. Two workshops a few minutes apart
can quote a two-fold difference on identical work, because one carries a spray booth, diagnostic
subscriptions and eight staff, and the other is three men and a hoist.

Both can be the right answer depending on the job. Neither is worth accepting without seeing the
other.

## Choosing by coverage, not by postcode

Between mobile teams working in basement car parks and workshops that collect from anywhere in
the city, the practical question is not which garage is near you. It is which garage does your
job well, at what price, with what warranty.
