Al Ain is inland, hot, and surrounded by desert, which produces a maintenance profile unlike the
coastal emirates.

## Heat without salt

Summer temperatures here run above the coast, and that load falls on two systems: cooling and air
conditioning.

Cooling first. A radiator, water pump or thermostat that would survive elsewhere is working
considerably harder here, and the failures that follow — overheating, warped heads, head gaskets
— are the most expensive on this site. Coolant condition and a cooling system pressure test are
worth including in an annual service rather than waiting for a temperature warning.

The compensation is that inland air carries no salt, so brake hardware, exhausts and underbody
components corrode far less than in Fujairah, Ajman or on the Palm.

## The AC point

Air conditioning here is not a comfort system. The most common cause of poor cooling inland is a
condenser packed with fine sand, which cripples heat rejection while the refrigerant system
itself is perfectly healthy.

That is a cleaning job rather than a parts job, and a garage that checks it before quoting a
compressor is saving you several thousand dirhams.

## Commuting

The Abu Dhabi and Dubai runs are long, and cars doing either daily accumulate motorway mileage
fast. Service on distance rather than annually, with an interim oil change between full services.

## Sand

Dune driving is common enough here to be part of routine maintenance. Afterwards: air filter,
underbody inspection, and reinflating tyres properly before returning to tarmac. Running
deflated on the motorway is both unsafe and hard on sidewalls.
