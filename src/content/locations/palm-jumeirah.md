The Palm's automotive problem is chemistry rather than mechanics. Cars here are surrounded by
sea on every side, and salt-laden humidity reaches everything.

## What corrodes, and why it matters

Brake calliper slider pins are the important one. When they seize, one pad does all the work, the
disc overheats unevenly, and you end up replacing discs that a AED 20 tube of grease would have
saved.

Exhaust clamps, hangers and underbody fasteners corrode too, which does not usually cause a
failure but makes every future repair longer and more expensive — a seized bolt is billed in
labour.

None of this is dramatic and all of it is preventable with two habits: regular washing including
an underbody rinse, and brake hardware that is cleaned and greased at every service rather than
simply measured.

## Paint

Salt haze combined with hard water and relentless sun is the worst combination for clear coat in
this city. It is why the automotive spend on the Palm skews so heavily to washing, coating and
film rather than mechanical work.

For a car being kept here, a ceramic coating earns its price more clearly than in almost any
other Dubai location — not because it looks good, but because it gives the paint something to
take the abuse instead.

## Logistics

One road in and out changes how collection works. Every workshop serving the Palm knows it;
agree a specific time rather than a window, and expect the collection run to be planned around
trunk traffic rather than around your convenience alone.
