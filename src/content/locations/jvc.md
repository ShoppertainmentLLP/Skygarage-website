JVC is one of Dubai's densest residential districts and one of its most awkward to drive out of,
which shapes how cars get looked after here more than any technical factor.

## The driving pattern

Low-speed running on the internal roads, then a queue at whichever exit you use. Repeated twice a
day.

That pattern is hard on specific components. Batteries, because short low-speed trips never
replace the charge that starting consumed. Brakes, because they work constantly at low
temperature and never clear themselves. And on CVT-equipped cars, the transmission, because stop
and go is exactly the condition that heats and degrades the fluid.

None of it is dramatic. It just means the calendar matters more than the odometer, and the CVT
fluid interval in particular is worth respecting on cars that have one.

## Why mobile wins here

The nearest real workshops are Al Quoz and Al Barsha, and neither is far in kilometres. The
problem is the twenty minutes spent getting out of JVC at either end of the day, twice, for a job
that takes an hour.

Mobile mechanics reach JVC easily and work in the building. For servicing, batteries, brakes,
diagnostics and washing, that is simply the better arrangement, and it is what most residents
here have settled on.

## Building rules

JVC ranges from towers with basements to low-rise blocks with surface parking, and the rules
differ. Surface parking is usually more permissive; basements almost always require a waterless
or steam wash rather than a hose.

Say which you have when booking and the team brings the right method.
