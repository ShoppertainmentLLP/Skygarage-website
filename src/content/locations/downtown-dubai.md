Downtown is dense, expensive and entirely without automotive trade, which means every car here is
looked after by someone who comes to it.

## The short-trip problem

Downtown driving is a few kilometres at a time: down the ramp, along Al Saada, into another
basement. It is the hardest possible pattern for a car.

The battery never fully recovers what starting used. The engine oil never reaches the temperature
that boils off moisture and fuel dilution. The brakes never get warm enough to clear the surface
film that forms in humidity.

The practical consequences are batteries failing at two years rather than three, oil that is in
worse condition than the mileage suggests, and brake discs that develop surface corrosion.

None of that argues for more servicing. It argues for servicing on time rather than on distance —
annually, whatever the odometer says.

## Valet parking

Convenient, and the single biggest source of cosmetic damage on cars in this district. Kerbed
rims and door dings accumulate steadily.

Both are inexpensive to correct if you deal with several at once. Paintless dent removal is
priced far better as a batch, and rim refurbishment in Al Quoz is straightforward with collection
from your building.

## How work actually happens here

Two options, both of which leave you where you are.

Mobile teams work in the basement for servicing, batteries, diagnostics and washing — subject to
your building's rules, which usually mean a waterless wash method and notice to security.

Or an Al Quoz or Ras Al Khor workshop collects the car in the morning and returns it the same
afternoon. Given how close both districts are, that is genuinely a same-day arrangement for
routine work.
