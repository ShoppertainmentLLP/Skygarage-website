Ras Al Khaimah has a driving environment that no other emirate shares, and it changes what cars
here need.

## The mountain factor

Jebel Jais is a sustained climb and, more importantly, a sustained descent. Long descents mean
long periods of braking, and that is a genuinely demanding condition — much harder on brakes than
anything in Dubai's flat traffic.

The component that matters most is the one nobody checks: brake fluid. It absorbs moisture over
time, which lowers its boiling point, and a long descent is exactly when boiling shows up — as a
pedal that suddenly goes to the floor.

Fluid condition is measurable with a meter in a couple of minutes, and on a car that regularly
drives the mountain it is worth checking annually rather than on a two-year cycle.

Engine braking rather than riding the brakes the whole way down does more for brake life than any
component upgrade.

## Distance

The emirate is spread out. That matters mostly for roadside assistance, where response times are
naturally longer than in a dense city, and for the practical decision of whether a car is
recovered locally or taken to Dubai for specialist work.

Recovery to a workshop of your choice anywhere in the UAE is straightforward, and for a specialist
job it is often the right call.

## Wadi and sand use

Common enough here to be part of routine maintenance rather than an exception. After a trip: an
underbody look, an air filter check, and attention to suspension bushes and brake hardware, all
of which take a beating from rocks, water and sand.
