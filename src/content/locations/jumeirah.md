Jumeirah's cars live outdoors, near the sea, and are usually kept for years. Each of those three
facts changes what maintenance actually means here.

## Sun on the paint

A car in a villa driveway takes UV every day, all year. Clear coat degrades, dashboards crack,
and interior plastics fade — all faster than on an identical car parked in a tower basement three
kilometres away.

The countermeasures are not exotic. Washing weekly, so dust is not sitting on the paint holding
overnight humidity against it. A windscreen shade, which does more for a dashboard than anything
else. And eventually, for a car being kept long term, a ceramic coating that gives the clear coat
something to hide behind.

## Salt

Jumeirah is close enough to the water that salt-laden air is a genuine factor, and it does its
work where you cannot see it: brake calliper slider pins, hardware, exhaust clamps and underbody
fasteners.

Seized slider pins cause uneven pad wear and warped discs, and they are entirely preventable by
cleaning and greasing the hardware at each brake service. It costs almost nothing and most
quick-service places skip it.

## Cars kept a long time

Households here keep vehicles longer than the Dubai average, often with the same workshop
throughout. That relationship is worth cultivating — a garage with your car's history makes
better decisions about what genuinely needs doing.

It also changes the maintenance calculus: on a car you intend to own for another five years,
paint protection, brake hardware servicing and fluid discipline all pay back. On a car you are
selling next year, they do not.
