Fujairah's driving conditions are the most demanding in the country, and its workshop trade is
the smallest. Both facts are worth planning around.

## Crossing the mountains

Any trip to or from Fujairah involves the Hajar range, which means sustained climbing followed by
sustained descent.

Climbing loads the cooling system: high engine output at relatively low road speed is exactly
when a marginal radiator, a tired water pump or a failing fan will show itself. Descending loads
the brakes, and specifically the brake fluid, which boils if it has absorbed moisture over the
years.

Neither is an exotic failure mode. Both are entirely preventable with a coolant check and a brake
fluid condition test before the trip, and both are unpleasant to discover on a pass.

## Humidity and corrosion

The east coast is noticeably more humid than the Gulf side, and humid salt air is harder on
metal and on electrical connections than dry heat is.

Corroded earth points and connectors are behind a large share of the intermittent electrical
faults seen here, and brake hardware seizes faster than inland. Cleaning and greasing brake
hardware at every service is the cheap habit that pays off.

## Working with a smaller trade

Fewer workshops and longer supply lines mean parts availability, not workshop capacity, is
usually what determines how long a job takes. A garage that confirms parts before dismantling the
car is worth choosing deliberately.

For genuinely specialist work, recovery to a Dubai or Sharjah workshop is straightforward and
frequently faster overall than waiting locally.
